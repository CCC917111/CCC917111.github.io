  • Long-Tail Driving Scene Discovery
    Spring 2026, capstone project with Zihua Chen and Yusong Zhao
    Rare, safety-critical driving scenes are hard to mine because they are often not visual outliers: a wheelchair user at a crossing looks like an ordinary street scene. We mined such scenes from the internal representations of a frozen vision-language model rather than from the images. A sparse autoencoder trained with a tail-guided objective decomposes the model's hidden state into a normal and a long-tail subspace; a frame is flagged when the long-tail subspace activates, and the units that fire explain why. On nuScenes this raised long-tail F1 from 0.02 for the same model prompted directly to 0.85, and individual units turned out to correspond to specific risk patterns such as rain, wheelchair users or pedestrians in construction zones, so they can serve as search keys over unlabelled data.

  • CNN-Mamba Forest Type Segmentation
    Fall 2025, course project with Qianhua Wan, Cherng Khai Hng and Jiawei Li
    Pixel-level forest type mapping from satellite data, where the hard part is telling natural forest from planted forest and tree crops rather than forest from non-forest. We built a multi-modal CNN-Mamba model that combines Sentinel-2 optical time series, climate variables and elevation: Mamba state-space blocks model the temporal sequence and the global spatial context in linear time, convolutional encoders capture local structure, a dynamic fusion module weights the three modalities per sample, and a U-Net-style decoder restores full resolution. On the ForTy benchmark, trained on only 12.5% of the data, it outperformed the UNet3D and UTAE baselines and was competitive with MTSViT.
