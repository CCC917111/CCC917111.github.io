Independent research

Strategic Adaptation of LLM Pricing Agents in Dynamic Markets

This project studies large language models as autonomous pricing agents. Two LLM sellers compete repeatedly in a simulated market whose demand expands and contracts over time, under a regulator that can flag their prices, ask for a revision or cap them.

Each seller justifies its price publicly and keeps private notes that it carries into later rounds, which makes it possible to follow how pricing strategies form and change as the market moves, how market history and persistent memory shape that adaptation, and when it produces supracompetitive or collusive pricing.

DWTS Voting Analysis: Hidden Support and Voting Rules

Audience votes are private, but judges’ scores and weekly eliminations are public. This study asks what those observations can reveal about fan support and how voting rules affect competition outcomes. Using 34 seasons of Dancing with the Stars, it combines Bayesian modeling with simulations of rank and percentage aggregation, judge saves and fan saves.

A separate chronological forecasting study compares score-based predictors and online expert mixtures, selecting model settings using earlier seasons. The experiments examine uncertainty, sensitivity and performance against simple baselines. The public software and follow-up experiments extend our original team study.

Zero-Shot Strategic Bidding in Coupled Electricity-Carbon Markets

A generation company must bid profitably under both an electricity price and a carbon price, which makes the physical cost calculation and the strategic markup two different problems. We separated them into two reasoning layers around an open-weight model, with rule-based verification between the layers, and supplied a sliding window of market feedback so that the bidding context is refined from round to round with no fine-tuning and no parameter update.

On the IEEE 14-bus system the agent earned higher average profit than a fixed-markup baseline while clearing in every round, and re-adjusted its bids under a sharp carbon-price shock.

LLM-CECM: Agent-Based Simulation of Coupled Electricity-Carbon Markets

A simulation framework in which generation companies are language-model agents bidding into an electricity market coupled to a carbon market. I built the simulation pipeline - data preprocessing, parallel agent rollout, market-state updates and visualisation - and ran the study on the IEEE 118-bus system.

Two components address the cost of such a simulation and the quality of the decisions in it: an adaptive decision frequency that lets agents skip full reasoning when the market state has not moved, cutting model calls and runtime by roughly 80% against a matched full-frequency baseline, and counterfactual reasoning that lets an agent evaluate bids it did not submit. In a congested-market experiment the framework roughly halved a rolling price-volatility measure.
