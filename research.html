First-author paper at IEEE PES APPEEC 2026

Zero-Shot Strategic Bidding in Coupled Electricity-Carbon Markets

How can a generator adapt its bids to changing electricity and carbon prices without retraining its language model?

A generation company must account for the cost of producing electricity and its carbon obligations, then decide what markup to bid. Those are different reasoning tasks: the cost calculation sets the physical basis of a bid, while the markup depends on the market the generator faces.

Separate the calculation from the strategy

We organized an open-weight model around two reasoning layers, separating physical cost calculation from strategic markup. Rule-based verification sits between the layers. A sliding window of market feedback supplies the context for the next bidding round, allowing the agent to revisit its strategy as conditions change. There is no fine-tuning or parameter update: adaptation takes place through the information available at inference time.

Evidence from a simulated market

On the IEEE 14-bus system, the agent earned higher average profit than a fixed-markup baseline while clearing in every round. It also readjusted its bids under a sharp carbon-price shock. The comparison is specific to the simulated IEEE 14-bus setting and the fixed-markup baseline.

DWTS Voting Analysis: Hidden Support and Voting Rules

What can public scores and eliminations reveal when audience votes remain private?

I lead the current project, which extends our team’s MCM study. The broader analysis covers 34 seasons of Dancing with the Stars, combining Bayesian fan-support modeling with comparisons of rank and percentage aggregation, judge saves and fan saves.

What the observations leave unresolved

A separate identification analysis finds that many fan-share allocations can be compatible with the same observed exit. Its feasible regions depend on a declared deterministic voting rule; they are distinct from posterior credible intervals. Recovering a single plausible allocation therefore does not establish the actual vote totals.

A simpler predictor was hard to improve on

A separate forecasting study evaluates 86 eligible outer-validation eliminations across 12 seasons. It uses current judges’ scores and earlier score histories, selecting settings on earlier seasons and freezing probabilities before revealing each outcome. A two-feature predictor had the best outer-validation point log loss: 1.5040, versus 1.5414 for current scores alone. The paired season-bootstrap interval for that difference spans zero, so the apparent gain remains uncertain. Extra online adaptation did not consistently improve on the simpler fixed mixture.

The forecasting evidence is retrospective, on previously examined data, and does not validate hidden audience votes. Independent prospective validation remains outstanding. The linked provenance distinguishes our original team research from the AI-assisted post-report software and experiments.

Research assistant; co-author in Renewable Energy

LLM-CECM: Agent-Based Simulation of Coupled Electricity-Carbon Markets

I built the simulation pipeline for this study: data preprocessing, parallel agent rollout, market-state updates and visualisation. I ran the study on the IEEE 118-bus system, where generation companies act as language-model agents bidding into an electricity market coupled to a carbon market.

Two components address the cost of such a simulation and the quality of the decisions in it: an adaptive decision frequency that lets agents skip full reasoning when the market state has not moved, cutting model calls and runtime by roughly 80% against a matched full-frequency baseline, and counterfactual reasoning that lets an agent evaluate bids it did not submit. In a congested-market experiment the framework roughly halved a rolling price-volatility measure.

Independent research

Strategic Adaptation of LLM Pricing Agents in Dynamic Markets

This ongoing project studies large language models as autonomous pricing agents. Two LLM sellers compete repeatedly in a simulated market whose demand expands and contracts over time, under a regulator that can flag their prices, ask for a revision or cap them.

Each seller justifies its price publicly and keeps private notes that it carries into later rounds, which makes it possible to follow how pricing strategies form and change as the market moves, how market history and persistent memory shape that adaptation, and when it produces supracompetitive or collusive pricing.
